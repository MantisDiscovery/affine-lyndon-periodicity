Imaginary standard Lyndon periodicity in untwisted affine types

Pranava Kumar, Abhijay Gangarapu

Paper Companion note Manuscript source Calculations GitHub BibTeX

Abstract

A fixed insertion law in every untwisted type

We prove that imaginary standard Lyndon words satisfy a fixed insertion formula in every untwisted affine type and every alphabet order, by establishing the remaining classical cases uniformly in the rank and combining them with the exceptional-type verification of Elkins and Tsymbaliuk. A type-independent phase argument identifies the exact increasing-chain preperiod with the initial standard prefix followed by a proper suffix of the period's Lyndon parent. This gives the bound $2|\delta|-2$ for its length and recovers the increasing positive system and its Dynkin parent tree from the two lowest imaginary degrees.

The classical argument represents real blocks as greatest extensions of chain or fork ideals. A first-endpoint comparison and a reflected raising lemma determine the word in degree $2\delta$, after which an existing propagation theorem gives every degree. We also give polynomial preprocessing in every untwisted type, with a sharper classical bound, and determine the primitive column lattices and zero-weight switch bounds in the classical models.

Definitions

Words that remember a basis position

Order the simple roots of an affine Lie algebra. A Lyndon word is a nonempty word smaller than each of its nonempty proper suffixes in the resulting lexicographic order. The standard Lyndon construction assigns selected words to positive root spaces and obtains a basis by recursive Lie brackets.

Real root spaces are one-dimensional, so a real root has one standard word. The imaginary root $k\delta$ has multiplicity equal to the finite rank $n$ in the untwisted case. We therefore list its words in decreasing order,

\[ \SL_1(k\delta)>\cdots>\SL_n(k\delta). \]

The index records a position in a Cartan flag as well as the degree. Controlling that position is what turns periodicity of real-root chains into the insertion formula of the main theorem.

Main result

Untwisted affine types

Fix any untwisted affine type of finite rank $n$, any order on its simple-root alphabet, and $1\leq i\leq n$. Write $\SL_i(\delta)=L_iR_i$ as a standard factorization.

Theorem 1.1 · Imaginary insertion There is a cyclic permutation $W_i$ of $\SL_j(\delta)$ for some $j\leq i$ such that, for every $k\geq1$,
\[ \SL_i(k\delta)=L_iW_i^{k-1}R_i,\qquad \SL_i^{\mathrm{ls}}(k\delta)=L_iW_i^{k-1}. \]
The theorem holds for every untwisted type and every alphabet order.

The new all-rank arguments handle the remaining classical types $B$, $C$, and $D$. The exceptional cases use the prior degree-$2\delta$ computations and propagation theorem of Elkins and Tsymbaliuk; this paper does not replace those computations with a new type-independent exceptional proof.

Corollary · Reconstruction from two degrees For a fixed untwisted affine root datum, the words in degrees $\delta$ and $2\delta$ determine the positive system of increasing real-root chains. They recover the finite Dynkin diagram, including bond multiplicities and orientations.

Classical reduction

The finite type-C block grid

When the least letter $r$ has Kac mark two, every degree-$k\delta$ word splits uniquely into $2k$ real blocks. In type $C_n^{(1)}$, the possible block degrees form a $2r$ by $2(n-r)$ rectangle. The grid contains all $4r(n-r)$ positive real roots whose $\alpha_r$-coefficient is one.

Exact phase

The period rotates; the preperiod records the cut

Let $B_i$ be the unique Lyndon rotation of $W_i$. There is a unique split $B_i=A_iC_i$ for which $W_i=C_iA_i$, with $0\leq|C_i|<|\delta|$. The increasing-chain preperiod is then $y_i=L_iC_i$, and the real appends are the canonical Lyndon factors of $C_i$.

Theorem 13.3 · Exact phase and preperiod For every untwisted type, $y_i=L_iC_i$ and $|y_i|\leq2|\delta|-2$. The repeating pattern begins at degree $\delta$ exactly when $C_i$ is empty, and at degree $2\delta$ otherwise.

Companion note

What extends to twisted types

The finite block alphabet and primitive-bracketing results hold for every indecomposable symmetrizable affine root system. Twisted imaginary multiplicities can vary with the degree, so four families cannot use the unchanged degree-$\delta$ indexing of the untwisted theorem.

Proved in $A_2^{(2)}$ With $\delta=\alpha_0+2\alpha_1$, the all-degree formulas for the two alphabet orders are
\[ 0<1:\quad \SL(k\delta)=01(011)^{k-1}1, \]
\[ 1<0:\quad \SL(k\delta)=1(110)^{k-1}10. \]

Established

Finite block alphabets, a nonzero bracketing for primitive zero-weight configurations, the twisted multiplicity obstruction, and the complete $A_2^{(2)}$ formula.

Conjectural

The divisibility-stratified insertion formula in the remaining twisted families. The recorded finite checks support it but do not prove propagation.

Scope

What the package does not claim

The main theorem covers every untwisted affine type. Its exceptional cases use cited prior computer-assisted verifications. A type-independent analytic replacement for those exceptional computations is not supplied.

The companion note proves the full insertion formula only for $A_2^{(2)}$ among twisted types. The proposed formulas for the other twisted families remain conjectural; finite calculations establish neither lexicographic maximality in every later flag layer nor the required propagation law.

The package also makes no quantum or shuffle-algebra analogue, categorification, canonical-basis statement, or full Gröbner-Shirshov presentation. The new arguments require coauthor review before submission, and no external submission or referee approval is recorded.
